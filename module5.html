⚖️ Life insurance: rent it or own it

⏱️ TERM — rent protection

Pure death benefit for a set period (10/20/30 yrs). Cheapest per dollar of coverage. No cash value — outlive it, nothing back. Perfect for big needs on a budget (young family!).

🏠 PERMANENT — own it

Whole life: fixed premium + guaranteed cash value. Universal: flexible premiums/death benefit. Variable: cash value in investment subaccounts (market risk!). Lasts for life — costs far more.

⚠️
Exam trap: If a question describes a young family needing maximum coverage at lowest cost → the answer is term. “Flexible premiums” → universal. “Investment subaccounts” → variable.

📉 See the cost over time

Drag through the years: term stays cheap but stops; whole costs far more yet is permanent and builds cash value.

Term is like renting protection — cheap while you need it most, then it ends. Whole life is like buying — much pricier, but it lasts your whole life and slowly builds a savings value inside.

🧮 How much life insurance? Two roads

💼 Human life value

Present value of the person's future earnings. “What paycheck stream disappears?”

📋 Needs analysis (preferred)

Add up actual needs — income replacement, mortgage, debts, education, final expenses — minus existing assets.

🤕 Disability insurance: protect the paycheck

👵 Long-term care & Medicare

🏡 Long-term care (LTC)

Covers help with activities of daily living (bathing, dressing, eating…) — usually triggered when you can't do 2 of 6 ADLs. Regular health insurance and Medicare do NOT meaningfully cover custodial care.

🏛️ Medicare's four parts

A = Hospital (“A for Admitted”) · B = Doctors/outpatient (“B for Bills from doctors”) · C = Medicare Advantage (private all-in-one) · D = Drugs. Eligibility at 65.
