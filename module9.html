🗂️ The estate planning toolbox

📜 Will

Says who gets what, names guardians for kids, names an executor. Dies without one = intestate → state law decides. A will still goes through probate.

⚖️ Probate

The court process validating a will and distributing assets. Public, slow, costly — much of estate planning is about avoiding it.

🤝 Durable power of attorney

Someone manages your finances if you're incapacitated. “Durable” = stays valid through incapacity.

🩺 Healthcare directives

Living will (your medical wishes) + healthcare proxy (who decides for you).

⚠️
Huge exam point: a beneficiary designation OVERRIDES the will. An outdated 401(k) beneficiary means an ex-spouse could inherit — no matter what the will says!

🎭 Trusts: revocable vs. irrevocable

🔄 Revocable (living) trust

You keep control, can change or cancel it. Avoids probate — but assets still count in your taxable estate and get no creditor shield.

🔒 Irrevocable trust

You give up control — permanently. In exchange: assets leave your taxable estate and gain creditor protection. Classic use: irrevocable life insurance trust (ILIT).

🧠
Memory hook: the three trust roles — Grantor gives, Trustee tends, Beneficiary benefits. “G.T.B. — Give, Tend, Benefit.”

♾️ Two spouse superpowers

💍 Unlimited marital deduction

Leave ANY amount to a U.S.-citizen spouse — zero estate tax at the first death.

📨 Portability

A surviving spouse can claim the deceased spouse's unused exemption (file the election!) — doubling the shield.

⚠️
Exam traps: (1) Inherited assets get a step-up in basis — heirs' capital gains clock resets to date-of-death value. (2) A revocable trust does NOT reduce estate taxes — it only avoids probate.
