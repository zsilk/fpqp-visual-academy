⏱️ The 3-hour game plan

🔪 How to attack a multiple-choice question

1

👀 Read the QUESTION before the story

Long scenario? Peek at the final sentence first — now you know what to hunt for in the story.

2

✍️ Answer in your head first

Predict before you look at choices — wrong options are DESIGNED to sound right.

3

❌ Eliminate ruthlessly

Cross off 2 obviously-wrong answers and a coin flip becomes 50/50 in your favor.

4

🚩 Beware absolute words

“Always,” “never,” “all,” “none” are usually wrong. “Generally” and “most” are usually right.

5

🦸 NEVER leave a blank

There's no penalty for guessing. A blank is a guaranteed zero; a guess is a free chance.

🪤 Spot the trap patterns

🌀 The “almost-twin”

Fee-based vs. fee-only · annuity due vs. ordinary · replacement cost vs. ACV · marginal vs. effective. When two answers look like twins, the exam is testing THAT exact difference — slow down there.

🔢 The “old number”

Outdated figures ($6,000 IRA, age 70½ RMDs) can appear as tempting wrong answers. You memorized the 2026 truth — trust it.

📏 The “wrong denominator”

Ratio questions swap gross vs. net income (remember: consumer debt 20% uses NET). Check what the ratio divides by before answering.

➕ The “EXCEPT” flip

“All of the following are true EXCEPT…” — underline EXCEPT in your mind and hunt for the FALSE one. Rushing past this word costs easy points.

🧠 The week before & the day of

📆 Final week

Retake every module quiz until each is 80%+. Review only your MISSED questions — that's where the points hide. Light review, heavy sleep.

🌅 Exam morning

Normal breakfast, water, arrive early, calculator ready (HP 10bII+). No new studying — it only shakes confidence.

💛 Mid-exam nerves?

Blank moment = normal. Breathe in 4, out 6, twice. Skip, flag, return. Remember: 70%, not 100% — you can miss 22 questions and still pass!

🌟
Read that last part again: you can miss 22 questions and STILL pass. And if the day goes sideways, you have a second attempt. The system is built for you to succeed — walk in like it. 🏆
