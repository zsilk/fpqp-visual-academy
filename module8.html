🏗️ The income tax waterfall

1

💵 Gross income

Everything taxable: wages, interest, business income, capital gains…

2

➖ Adjustments (“above-the-line”)

Deductible IRA/HSA contributions, student loan interest, etc. → gives you AGI.

3

➖ Standard OR itemized deduction

2026 standard deduction: $16,100 single · $32,200 married filing jointly · $24,150 head of household. Itemize only if bigger.

4

= Taxable income → apply the brackets

Seven rates for 2026: 10, 12, 22, 24, 32, 35, 37%.

5

➖ Credits → final tax bill

Credits cut the TAX itself, dollar for dollar. That's why credits beat deductions.

🪜 Marginal vs. effective: the staircase

⚠️
Exam trap: “A raise pushed me into the 24% bracket, so ALL my income is taxed at 24%” — FALSE. Only the dollars inside the 24% band are.

🎚️ See it stack: drag an income

Slide the income and watch each bracket fill up. Notice your effective rate always trails your marginal rate — because only the top slice hits the top rate.

Your income fills buckets from the bottom up. Only the money that reaches a higher bucket pays that higher rate — a raise never re-taxes the money below it.

⏳ Capital gains: the 1-year stopwatch

🏃 Short-term (held ≤ 1 year)

Taxed at your ordinary income rates — up to 37%. Ouch.

🧘 Long-term (held > 1 year)

Preferential rates: 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income. Patience literally pays.

🎯 Deduction vs. credit: which is worth more?

✂️ Deduction

Reduces taxable income. A $1,000 deduction saves $1,000 × your bracket (e.g., $220 in the 22% bracket).

💎 Credit (the winner)

Reduces the tax bill itself. A $1,000 credit saves the full $1,000 — regardless of bracket.

🪣 Tax buckets to recognize on sight

Tax-DEFERRED: traditional 401(k)/IRA, annuities Tax-FREE: Roth withdrawals, muni bond interest, HSA (medical) Taxable now: wages, interest, short-term gains Step-up in basis: inherited assets (heirs' gains reset)
🧠
Memory hook: Muni bonds = federal-tax-free interest — which is why they typically pay LOWER stated rates and shine for high-bracket clients. Compare using the taxable-equivalent yield: muni rate ÷ (1 − tax bracket).
