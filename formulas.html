⏳ Time value of money

ConceptFormulaPlain English / worked example
Future value (lump sum)FV = PV × (1 + i)ⁿGrowing one deposit forward. $25,000 at 7% for 12 years annually → $56,304.79.
Present value (lump sum)PV = FV ÷ (1 + i)ⁿDiscounting a future amount back to today. Need $80,000 in 6 yrs at 5% → deposit $59,697.23 now.
FV of an ordinary annuityFV = PMT × [((1+i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i]Saving $500/mo for 30 yrs at 6% (monthly) → $502,257.52 (only ~36% of that is your own contributions).
PV of an ordinary annuityPV = PMT × [(1 − (1+i)⁻ⁿ) ÷ i]Wanting $40,000/yr for 25 retirement years at 5% → need $563,757.78 at the start.
Annuity due (either direction)ordinary-annuity result × (1 + i)Payments at the START of the period (rent, leases) are always worth more than the same ordinary annuity.
Rule of 72years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rateFast mental estimate. At 8%: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 yrs (precise answer is 9.01 yrs).
Periodic rate & periodsi = annual rate ÷ periods per yr; n = years × periods per yr6% annual, monthly compounding, 30 yrs → i = 0.5, n = 360. Convert BOTH or neither — a classic slip.
Effective annual rate (EAR)EAR = (1 + i/m)ᵐ − 1Comparing rates with different compounding frequencies apples-to-apples.

📐 Cash-flow & debt ratios

RatioFormulaLimit / worked example
Basic liquidity ratioliquid assets ÷ monthly non-discretionary outflows3–6 months. $18,000 ÷ $5,200 = 3.5 months.
Current ratiocurrent assets ÷ current liabilitiesBelow 1.0 = can't cover this year's bills from this year's liquid resources.
Savings ratio(savings + employer match) ÷ gross income10–20%+, rising the later you start. $760 ÷ $9,500 = 8.0% (below target).
Housing (front-end)PITI ÷ gross monthly income≤ 28%. $2,400 ÷ $9,500 = 25.3%.
Total debt (back-end)all debt payments ÷ gross monthly income≤ 36%. $3,300 ÷ $9,500 = 34.7%.
Consumer debtnon-housing debt ÷ NET income≤ 20% (under 10% preferred). $900 ÷ $7,100 = 12.7%.
Debt-to-total-assetstotal liabilities ÷ total assetsA leverage measure — naturally high when young, should fall with age.
⚠️
Watch the switch: the 20% consumer-debt ratio uses net (take-home) income, while the front-end/back-end/savings ratios use gross.

☂️ Insurance math

ConceptFormulaPlain English / worked example
Coinsurance (80% rule)payout = (carried ÷ required) × loss$400k replacement cost home carrying only $240k (required $320k), $60,000 loss → $60,000 × ($240k÷$320k) = $45,000 paid, client eats the rest.
Actual cash valueACV = replacement cost − depreciationWhat older-property claims and auto comp/collision settle at — no bonus for a brand-new replacement.
Needs-based life insuranceNeed = Total needs − Available resourcesFinal expenses + debt + PV of income replacement + education + reserve, minus existing coverage & assets. Whitfield case: ≈$850,000 shortfall for Marcus.
Human life value (quick estimate)PV of future earnings, net of taxes & self-consumptionA rough alternative to the needs-based method — simpler, less precise.
Disability benefit, after-taxgross benefit × (1 − effective tax rate)Employer-paid group LTD is taxable: a "60% benefit" at a 22% rate nets roughly 47% of gross pay, not 60%.
Health-plan worst casedeductible + out-of-pocket max = total exposure$3,000 deductible + $7,000 OOP max plan on $60,000 of charges → insured pays exactly $7,000, plan pays $53,000.
Annuity exclusion ratioinvestment in contract ÷ expected total return$90,000 basis, $1,000/mo for 20 yrs (expected return $240,000) → exclusion ratio 37.5%; $375 of each payment is tax-free, $625 taxable.

📈 Investment & return math

ConceptFormulaPlain English / worked example
Total return (stock)(ending value − beginning value + income) ÷ beginning valueBought at $50, $2 dividend, ends at $54 → ($54−$50+$2)÷$50 = 12.0% (price-only would show just 8%).
Tax-adjusted return 📄 givenreturn × (1 − tax rate)9% return at a 22% rate → 7.02% after tax.
Inflation-adjusted (real) return 📄 given[(1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 17% nominal, 3% inflation → exact real rate = 3.88% (not the sloppy "7−3=4%" shortcut).
Tax- & inflation-adjusted return 📄 given[(1 + tax-adj. return) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 19% nominal, 22% tax, 3% inflation → 7.02% after tax → 3.90% real. The headline 9% delivers under 4% of real purchasing power.
Taxable-equivalent yieldmuni rate ÷ (1 − tax bracket)3.5% muni, 32% bracket → 3.5%÷0.68 = 5.15% taxable-equivalent.
Current yield (bond)annual coupon ÷ current market price5% coupon ($50/yr) on a $1,000 bond trading at $900 → $50÷$900 = 5.56% (trading at a discount).
Sharpe ratio(return − risk-free rate) ÷ std. deviationExcess return per unit of TOTAL risk. Fund A: 12%/20% SD, risk-free 3% → 0.45. Fund B: 9%/10% SD → 0.60 — Fund B wins risk-adjusted despite the lower raw return.
Treynor ratio(return − risk-free rate) ÷ betaSame idea using beta — excess return per unit of SYSTEMATIC risk; use for one holding inside a diversified portfolio.
Net asset value (NAV)(fund assets − fund liabilities) ÷ shares outstandingComputed once daily; mutual fund orders fill at the NEXT computed NAV ("forward pricing").

🧾 Tax & retirement

ConceptFormulaPlain English / worked example
Effective tax ratetotal tax ÷ total incomeAlways lower than the marginal (top) rate. $8,260 tax on $60,000 income = 13.77% effective vs. a 22% marginal bracket.
Credit vs. deduction valuecredit = full $ value; deduction = $ × marginal rateAt 22%: a $1,000 credit saves $1,000; a $1,000 deduction saves only $220.
The AGI flowGross income − adjustments = AGI; AGI − std/itemized ded. − QBI = taxable incomeAGI is a gatekeeper — it drives IRA/Roth eligibility, NIIT, IRMAA, and dozens of phaseouts.
Capital gain / lossamount realized − adjusted basisSold at $46,800 (net), basis $28,150 → $18,650 long-term gain.
Required minimum distribution 📄 givenprior 12/31 balance ÷ life-expectancy factorYearly RMD once you reach age 73 or 75, depending on birth year.
Provisional income (Social Security)AGI + tax-exempt interest + 50% of Social Security benefitsCompared to thresholds to find how much of SS (0/50/85%) is taxable. Muni interest is added back — it doesn't shelter this.

🏰 Estate & transfer math

ConceptFormulaPlain English / worked example
Taxable estategross estate − debts/admin costs − marital ded. − charitable ded.Add back post-1976 adjusted taxable gifts, then apply rates and the unified credit.
Step-up in basisnew basis = fair market value at date of deathA $50,000 asset worth $400,000 at death passes with a $400,000 basis — lifetime gain escapes income tax entirely.
Gift-tax annual exclusiongifts above the exclusion draw down the lifetime exemptionUnused annual exclusions don't carry over — "use it or lose it" every year.
