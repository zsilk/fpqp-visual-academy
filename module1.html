🗺️ The 7-step journey

Picture this as a road trip with a client. You can't skip a stop. The exam LOVES asking what happens at each step and in what order.

1

🤝 Understand the client's personal & financial circumstances

Meet them. Gather facts (numbers) and feelings (values, fears, dreams).

2

🎯 Identify & select goals

Turn “I want to be okay someday” into specific, prioritized goals.

3

🔎 Analyze the current course & alternatives

Will their current path reach the goals? What else could work better?

4

📋 Develop the recommendations

Choose specific actions, products, and strategies that fit THIS client.

5

🗣️ Present the recommendations

Explain clearly so the client truly understands and can decide.

6

🚀 Implement

Do the things: open accounts, buy coverage, retitle assets, automate savings.

7

🔄 Monitor progress & update

Life changes — so plans must too. Review and adjust on a schedule.

🧠
Memory hook: “Unicorns In Art Draw Pretty Incredible Murals” → Understand, Identify, Analyze, Develop, Present, Implement, Monitor. Silly = sticky!

🎯 Goals that actually work: PTA

A usable goal needs all three ingredients. If a question shows a vague goal, it's missing one of these:

💡 P — Purpose

WHAT is the money for? “Retirement income” beats “some money later.”

⏰ T — Time frame

WHEN is it needed? “In 12 years” beats “eventually.”

💰 A — Amount

HOW MUCH? “$40,000/year” beats “enough to be comfortable.”

📊 Two kinds of client data

🔢 Quantitative = countable

Facts and numbers: income, account balances, debts, ages, insurance coverage, tax returns.

Quantity → numbers.

💗 Qualitative = qualities

Feelings and values: risk tolerance, health, family dynamics, dreams, fears, priorities.

Quality → how they feel.

⚖️ The BIG one: standards of care

Who must put the client first, and how much? This changed since your book — here's the 2026 picture:

🛡️ Fiduciary standard

Investment advisers (RIAs) and CFP® professionals giving financial advice must act in the client's best interest at all times — duty of loyalty, duty of care, follow client instructions.

🤝 Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)

Broker-dealers are no longer just “suitability.” Since June 2020, when recommending to retail customers they must act in the customer's best interest and meet 4 obligations.

⚠️
Exam trap: “Fee-based” ≠ “fee-only.” Fee-based advisers can ALSO earn commissions. If the question hinges on fewest conflicts of interest, the answer is fee-only.

🏛️ Who regulates whom?

🦅 SEC

Federal securities regulator. Oversees larger investment advisers (RIAs), public markets, and enforces Reg BI.

🛡️ FINRA

Industry self-regulator for broker-dealers and their registered reps. Licensing exams, conduct rules, discipline.

🏠 State regulators (NASAA)

Register smaller advisers and enforce state securities laws. Series 65/66 exams qualify adviser reps.

☂️ State insurance commissioners (NAIC)

Insurance is regulated by the states, coordinated through the NAIC. Agents need state licenses.
