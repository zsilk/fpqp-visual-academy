🧩 Owner or lender?

📈 Stock = you're an OWNER

A slice of the company. You may get dividends and price growth — or losses. Common stock votes; preferred gets fixed dividends first (bond-like).

🧾 Bond = you're a LENDER

An IOU. You get coupon interest + your money back at maturity. Safer than stock of the same company — bondholders are paid first if things go wrong.

🧠
The seesaw you MUST know: interest rates ⬆️ → existing bond prices ⬇️ (and vice versa). Longer maturity = bigger swings. Picture a seesaw with “rates” on one side and “prices” on the other. ⚖️

🎢 The risk–return ladder

🎭 The two families of risk

🌎 Systematic — market-wide

Hits EVERYTHING: recessions, interest rates, inflation, war. Diversification cannot remove it. Measured by beta.

🏢 Unsystematic — one company

A CEO scandal, a factory fire, one bad product. Diversification CAN nearly eliminate it — that's the whole point of owning many holdings.

⚠️
Exam trap: “Which risk can diversification eliminate?” → unsystematic (company-specific). Beta measures systematic risk; standard deviation measures total risk.

🥄 Strategies that tame timing

📆 Dollar-cost averaging

Invest a fixed amount on a schedule. You automatically buy MORE shares when prices are low, fewer when high.

🪴 Diversification

Spread across assets whose returns don't move together (low correlation) — smooths the ride.

⚖️ Rebalancing

Periodically sell winners / buy laggards back to target weights. Forces “buy low, sell high.”

📏 Quick measures to recognize

Beta — sensitivity to the market (1.0 = market) Standard deviation — total volatility Sharpe ratio — return earned per unit of risk Correlation — how two assets move together (+1 to −1)
